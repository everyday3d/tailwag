# Changelog

All notable changes to **TailWag Studio** will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Choice of relief in Manual mode (#41)**: A new "Relief in Manual mode" switch lets you pick **Match photo** (after you let go of a slider, every part of the picture re-picks the layer that best matches your photo for the stack you built) or **Fixed relief** (the relief keeps the shape from the photo's brightness and sliders only move where colours change; nothing is re-solved). Your choice is remembered.

### Fixed
- **Manual mode no longer re-solves while you adjust (#41)**: Dragging a layer slider used to rebuild the whole relief on every step, changing the height of about 19% of the picture for one 3-layer move, and cost about 157 ms per step. The relief now holds still while you drag and updates once when you let go (or not at all with Fixed relief).
- **Manual mode keeps your swap layers (#41)**: Changing a spool, reordering slots, removing or adding a slot, editing a spool in the stack, and the Base Layer Thickness slider no longer move boundaries you set by hand. They are only changed where needed to stay valid, and a new slot splits its neighbour's range. Switching from an automatic mode still re-tunes once, as before.
- The timeline badge says "Manual: your layers stay put" in Manual mode instead of "Auto-Tune & Re-Render", and the Auto-Tune Swap Layers button now follows the Faithful/Vivid setting.

- **Faithful Palette Search (Spools, Order and Swap Layers)** (`src/engine/faithfulSolver.ts`, `src/App.tsx`): In Faithful mode the auto-solver searches which spools to use, in what order and where they swap, by simulated annealing over the library (shortlisted to the colors the photo needs) ranked with a fast metric, then refines the swap layers exactly and decides with standard ΔE00. It is deterministic for a given seed, skips spools marked out of stock, avoids two filaments that look alike, discourages a see-through base, and takes about 0.6 to 1.7 seconds. On four real photographs with the 36-spool personal library, the mean color difference versus the earlier solver's spools (with swap layers re-optimized) fell from 12.9, 12.0, 9.8 and 8.6 to 7.3, 8.1, 8.4 and 6.6 with 4 colors, and from 13.1, 10.4, 14.0 and 8.9 to 6.0, 7.0, 7.1 and 5.8 with 8 colors. The earlier recipes stay in the list after it (Refs #28).

- **Translucent Base Layers in the Optical Model** (`src/engine/opticalModel.ts`, `src/engine/opticalTdEngine.ts`, `src/engine/colorMatch.ts`, `src/engine/printPlan.ts`): The first layers are no longer treated as a solid color by decree. Every layer, including the initial layer, is a translucent layer over the backing (a dark surface by default), so a clear or white base filament looks gray instead of white, which the palette search had been exploiting by choosing a transparent spool as the base. The model id is now `km-linear-v2` (Refs #27).

- **Faithful Color Matching: Best-Match Heights & Optimal Swap Layers (default)** (`src/engine/colorMatch.ts`, `src/engine/cadMeshBuilder.ts`, `src/engine/autoSolver.ts`, `src/store/useTailWagStore.ts`, `src/App.tsx`): In Faithful mode each column's height is no longer derived from luminance; it takes the stack depth whose predicted color is closest to the photo's own color (standard CIEDE2000, no saturation boost, every printed column counted equally), with an edge-aware smoothness term so equal colors do not scatter across the stack and a small anchor to the previous luminance height as tie-breaker. Heights land exactly on layer boundaries, so slicers have nothing to round. Swap layers are chosen by searching every monotone set of boundaries (exhaustive up to 4000 candidates, otherwise coordinate descent from several starts), ranked with a fast metric and decided with ΔE00, with at least 2 layers per filament and locked slots respected; the current boundaries are always a candidate so the search never returns something worse. A dynamic program does not apply because a layer's color depends on everything below it. On four real photographs with the existing 4-color recipes, the mean color difference to the photo fell from 19.7, 15.6, 14.3 and 17.0 (luminance mapping) to 13.6, 12.3, 10.8 and 10.2 with best-match heights, and to 13.1, 12.1, 9.8 and 8.7 once the swap layers were re-optimized. A **Faithful / Vivid** toggle in the toolbar keeps the earlier saturation-boosted luminance mapping available; switching re-tunes the swap layers (Refs #28).

- **Unified Optical Model, Standard CIEDE2000 & Accuracy Preview** (`src/engine/opticalModel.ts`, `src/engine/colorScience.ts`, `src/engine/opticalTdEngine.ts`, `src/engine/colorQuantizerEngine.ts`, `src/engine/printPlan.ts`, `src/components/Viewport3D.tsx`, `src/App.tsx`, `src/store/useTailWagStore.ts`, `mcp/server.ts`): The layer color table, and with it the solver, the viewport, the print plan and every export, now use one physical model: a two-flux Kubelka-Munk layer model evaluated per color channel in linear light, replacing the gamma-space heuristic with its magic constants. TD has an exact meaning in the model (diffuse transmittance falls to 10% at a thickness of TD) and every profile is marked as an estimate until the calibration phase fits it from a printed plate. One correct sRGB/XYZ/Lab implementation (`colorScience.ts`) replaces the rounded one, and the unused CIE76 distance was removed: `deltaE` is now standard CIEDE2000, verified against the Sharma reference data. The solver keeps its hue-weighted variant under the old name, clearly separated from the standard metric that is reported. A new **Accuracy** preview (default) shows the unlit predicted color of every column; **Beauty** keeps studio lighting. The plan hash and metadata now include the optical model id. Solver regression thresholds were re-baselined because the physical model gives honestly different scores (Refs #27).

- **3MF Round-Trip Verifier That Blocks Wrong Exports** (`src/engine/verifyExport.ts`, `src/engine/verifiedExport.ts`, `src/engine/slicerSettings.ts`, `src/components/ExportModal.tsx`): After a file is built, it is re-read and compared with the print plan, and the export is blocked with an explanation if anything differs; there is no override. The STL is checked the same way (header plan ID and every column height), and the no-AMS bundle also checks the plan and metadata files and that every pause layer and Z appears in the guides. Warnings (columns within 0.001 mm of a layer mid-plane, open or flipped mesh edges) are shown but do not block. The Export dialog shows a verified note or the blocking reasons (Fixes #26).

### Fixed
- **3D Viewport Colors Were Double Gamma-Encoded (Too Light and Washed Out)** (`src/components/Viewport3D.tsx`): Predicted sRGB colors were written straight into Three.js vertex colors, which are treated as linear light and encoded to sRGB on output, so every color looked lighter than its hex value. - **3MF Band Meshes Lost Material Where Slopes Cross Filament Boundaries** (`src/engine/bandMesh.ts`, `src/engine/exporter3mf.ts`): Each filament band was built by dropping every quad whose four corners were not all above the band floor, so material on a slope that crosses a boundary belonged to no band. On a test image the 3MF solid was about 3% smaller than the STL and heightmap, and peaks crossing a band ceiling lost their tops. Bands are now cut exactly against their floor and ceiling planes with edge intersections computed from the original triangle edge, so the parts tile the full solid and meet on flat interfaces. A test shows the band volumes add up to the whole solid (Fixes #33).

- **Landscape Spatial Denoising, Bilateral Heightmap Filtering, Gradient Continuity & Restrictive Dominance Pruning** (`src/engine/cadMeshBuilder.ts`, `src/engine/layerSliceConverter.ts`, `src/engine/autoSolver.ts`, `src/engine/colorQuantizerEngine.ts`, `src/engine/hueForgeAutoEngine.ts`): Eliminated splotchy noise, erratic height spikes, and fragmented color assignments in high-detail landscape photos:
  1. *Spatial Coherence / Bilateral Filtering on Height Maps*: Implemented `applyBilateralHeightmapFilter` ($\sigma_s = 1.8$, $\sigma_r = 0.08$, $5\times 5$ window) into CAD mesh grid generation, layer slicing, and auto-engine luminance processing, smoothing random background micro-spikes (leaves, gravel, pavement roughness) while preserving sharp step edges and silhouettes.
  2. *Gradient Preservation & Banding Prevention*: Added `computeGradientBandingPenalty` and adjacent micro-layer banding prevention ($< 2$ layers / $0.16\text{mm}$) into the Beer-Lambert stack evaluation cost function, disallowing abrupt contrasting filament swaps across adjacent micro-layers in smooth scenic gradients (like canopy transitions and road surfaces) unless backed by a sharp structural silhouette edge ($\text{edgeStrength} \ge 0.35$).
  3. *Restrictive Cluster Dominance for Palette Selection*: Enforced regional cluster dominance ($\ge 2\%$ of active image area), automatically merging non-dominant low-chroma micro-clusters into the nearest dominant midtone to prevent stray speckling (such as random dark/green spots on asphalt roads) while strictly preserving intentional ultra-saturated graphic accents ($\text{chroma} \ge 45$ and $\ge 0.35\%$ area) (Fixes #12).
- **Morphological Region Merging, Discrete Layer Quantization Banding & Structural Gradient Smoothing** (`src/engine/cadMeshBuilder.ts`, `src/engine/layerSliceConverter.ts`, `src/engine/hueForgeAutoEngine.ts`): Eliminated high-frequency pixel speckling and digital static across the height-map and layer slicing pipeline:
